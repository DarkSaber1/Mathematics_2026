# Exercise 14 – Cone and Cylinder

## Problem Statement

Compare

$$
x^2+y^2=z^2
$$

and

$$
x^2+y^2=4.
$$

For each surface, describe the cross-sections $z=c$, $x=0$, and $y=0$, make a sketch, and explain why the radius of the cross-section depends on $z$ in the first case but is constant in the second.

## Solution

### 1. First Surface: $x^2 + y^2 = z^2$ (Circular Double Cone)

#### Cross-Sections
- **Horizontal Cross-Sections $z = c$:**  
  Substituting $z = c$ gives[cite: 1]:

$$
x^2 + y^2 = c^2
$$

  - For $c \neq 0$: a **circle** centered at $(0, 0, c)$ with radius $r(c) = \sqrt{c^2} = |c|$, growing linearly as $|c|$ increases.
  - For $c = 0$: $x^2 + y^2 = 0$, which degenerates into the **single vertex point** $(0, 0, 0)$.
- **Vertical Cross-Section $x = 0$ (in the $yz$-plane):**  
  Setting $x = 0$ gives[cite: 1]:

$$
y^2 = z^2 \implies z = \pm y
$$

  This consists of **two intersecting straight lines** ($z = y$ and $z = -y$) crossing at the origin $(0, 0, 0)$ at a $45^\circ$ angle to the axes.
- **Vertical Cross-Section $y = 0$ (in the $xz$-plane):**  
  Setting $y = 0$ gives[cite: 1]:

$$
x^2 = z^2 \implies z = \pm x
$$

  This also consists of **two intersecting straight lines** ($z = x$ and $z = -x$) crossing at $(0, 0, 0)$.

#### Qualitative Sketch Description
The surface is an infinite **double circular cone** symmetric around the $z$-axis with its apex (pinch point) at $(0, 0, 0)$. Two identical conical nappes open linearly upward ($z > 0$) and downward ($z < 0$) at a $45^\circ$ angle from the $z$-axis, meeting only at the origin.

---

### 2. Second Surface: $x^2 + y^2 = 4$ (Right Circular Cylinder)

#### Cross-Sections
- **Horizontal Cross-Sections $z = c$:**  
  Because $z$ does not appear in $x^2 + y^2 = 4$, setting $z = c$ leaves the equation completely unchanged for every $c \in \mathbb{R}$[cite: 1]:

$$
x^2 + y^2 = 4
$$

  Every horizontal slice at any height $z = c$ is an identical **circle** centered at $(0, 0, c)$ with constant radius $r = \sqrt{4} = 2$.
- **Vertical Cross-Section $x = 0$ (in the $yz$-plane):**  
  Setting $x = 0$ gives[cite: 1]:

$$
y^2 = 4 \implies y = \pm 2
$$

  This consists of **two parallel vertical straight lines** ($y = 2$ and $y = -2$) running infinitely along the $z$-axis in the $yz$-plane.
- **Vertical Cross-Section $y = 0$ (in the $xz$-plane):**  
  Setting $y = 0$ gives[cite: 1]:

$$
x^2 = 4 \implies x = \pm 2
$$

  This also consists of **two parallel vertical straight lines** ($x = 2$ and $x = -2$) running infinitely along the $z$-axis in the $xz$-plane.

#### Qualitative Sketch Description
The surface is an infinite **vertical hollow cylinder** (tube) of constant radius $2$ centered along the $z$-axis. Every vertical slice through the $z$-axis produces two parallel walls spaced $4$ units apart, and every horizontal slice produces the exact same circle of radius $2$.

---

### 3. Why the Radius Depends on $z$ in the Cone but Is Constant in the Cylinder

In cylindrical coordinates, the horizontal distance from any point $(x, y, z)$ to the $z$-axis is $r = \sqrt{x^2 + y^2}$, so $x^2 + y^2 = r^2$:

- **In the cone $x^2 + y^2 = z^2$:**  
  The variable $z$ is explicitly coupled to $x^2 + y^2$, giving $r^2 = z^2 \implies r = |z|$[cite: 1]. Because $z$ actively participates in the equation, changing height $z$ changes the right-hand side of the circle equation, forcing the horizontal radius $r$ to scale linearly with vertical distance $|z|$ from the origin.
- **In the cylinder $x^2 + y^2 = 4$:**  
  The variable $z$ is **absent** from the equation (its coefficient is $0$), giving $r^2 = 4 \implies r = 2$ regardless of $z$[cite: 1]. Because no condition restricts $z$, if $(x, y, 0)$ lies on the base circle $x^2 + y^2 = 4$, then $(x, y, z)$ satisfies the equation for *every* $z \in \mathbb{R}$. Geometrically, the surface is generated by translating the circle $x^2 + y^2 = 4$ vertically along the $z$-axis without scaling, keeping the radius strictly constant at $2$.
